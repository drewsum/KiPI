KiPI vs HyperLynx: Glasgow revC3

The same board solved in KiPI and in HyperLynx PI. For DC drop, the same loads are compared pin by pin: the copper solutions agree to within HyperLynx's printed resolution, and the differences that remain come from how each tool treats series parts and ground. For the same heat, both tools put the hot spot on FB1, and KiPI's board runs warmer. For AC impedance, the +3V3 rail is compared at the FPGA, U30: the two agree in the capacitive range and part above about 300 kHz, because HyperLynx was told the capacitors' ESL includes their mounting and KiPI simulates the mounting on top. With the capacitors attached at their vias, as HyperLynx treats them, KiPI's inductance matches.

The solver guide explains how KiPI's DC and electro-thermal solvers work. The user guide explains the setup and results.

  • The copper solutions agree. On +3V3, the +1V2 core and VIO_AUX, every pin agrees within 0.07 mV. Each tool's voltage is measured from its own regulator's pins. HyperLynx prints voltages to 0.1 mV.
  • Series parts read 2.0 % higher in HyperLynx. All 8 parts read ×1.020 to ×1.021, wherever they sit on the board and whatever current they carry. That is copper's temperature coefficient taken from 20 °C to the 25 °C ambient. KiPI uses the resistance it is given, unchanged. With KiPI's series resistances scaled by 1.020, every power pin agrees within 0.13 mV.
  • KiPI's board runs warmer for the same heat. Both tools heat the board with the copper and series-part losses only, about 54 mW, almost all of it in FB1 and U15. Both put the hot spot on FB1. KiPI peaks at 31.7 °C on F.Cu against HyperLynx's 28.5 °C, and the rest of its board sits at 25.5 to 26.6 °C against 25.0 to 25.3 °C.
  • AC impedance at U30: the difference above 300 kHz is the capacitors' mounting. With identical capacitor models, both tools see 17.80 µF on +3V3, and |Z| agrees within 1.5 % from 1 kHz to 170 kHz. Above that KiPI has more inductance: its first series resonance is lower (0.86 against 1.14 MHz) and above 100 MHz it reads 0.63 against 0.31 nH. HyperLynx's models say their ESL already includes the mounting (INCLUDES_MOUNTING=1), so it leaves out the pads, fanout traces and vias, which KiPI simulates on top of the ESL. Attaching each capacitor at its vias in KiPI gives 0.29 nH and a first resonance at 1.14 MHz. See AC impedance.
  • Ground is modelled differently. HyperLynx holds every regulator's ground pins at 0 V. KiPI lets a linear regulator's ground pins float, and returns all load current to the board's sources. KiPI's ground near the regulators and the FPGA sits 0.18–0.28 mV higher as a result.

By analysis

HyperLynx reports each regulator's rail as its own analysis. The table gives the largest |ΔV| in each one.

Key pins

Series parts

The series parts are ferrite beads, a load switch and resistors, each given a DC resistance in the BUD. KiPI's drop across each part is exactly current × resistance. HyperLynx's drop is 2.0 % higher, by nearly the same ratio for every part. The last column but one gives the temperature at which copper's temperature coefficient (0.00393/K from 20 °C) would produce the measured ratio: 25.0 to 25.3 °C for every part, the ambient. That holds even for FB1, which HyperLynx's own map puts at 28.5 °C, so HyperLynx scales the series parts to the ambient, not to the board temperature under them.

Ground

The two tools model ground differently:

  • HyperLynx solves each regulator's rail as a separate analysis. Every regulator's ground pins are held at 0 V and take that rail's return current.
  • KiPI solves one ground carrying every current. A linear regulator's ground pins float and draw only its quiescent current. Load current returns to the board's sources, the USB connector J1 and the VIO_AUX supply pin J5.43, which are both held at 0 V.

KiPI holds J1 and J5.43 at 0 V together, so the two behave as if they were joined off the board. As a result, 115 mA of return current leaves through J5.43, although VIO_AUX itself supplies only 5 mA.

Positive current leaves the ground net at that pin. HyperLynx's values come from the analysis named in the last column, and each is relative to that analysis's own 0 V.

Via currents

These are HyperLynx's highest-current vias, each matched to the KiPI via at the same position. On most vias the two tools agree within a few percent. KiPI reads about 10 % lower on the highest-current +5V vias next to the regulator inputs. The two 0.1 A entries for VIOA and VIOB are connector pin holes, which KiPI reports as pins, not vias.

Thermal

Both tools heat the board with the same sources: the copper and via losses and the I²R of the 8 series parts, about 54 mW in all, 38 mW of it in FB1 and U15. Neither includes the regulators' conversion loss or the loads' power. Both put the hot spot on FB1, at the left edge of the board. KiPI runs warmer: its peak on F.Cu is 3.2 °C higher, and the rest of its board sits about 0.4 to 1.3 °C above HyperLynx's, which stays within 0.3 °C of ambient away from FB1.

AC impedance: +3V3 at U30

The impedance of the +3V3 rail looking into the FPGA, U30: its five +3V3 balls (C10, D6, F8, H6, L11) against its ten GND balls (E5–E7, F5–F7, G5–G7, H5), from 1 kHz to 300 MHz. The regulators carry no impedance in either tool, so below the capacitors' resonances |Z| is set by the total capacitance alone.

  • Capacitive range (to about 300 kHz): agreement. The capacitor models are identical in both tools, and so is the capacitance they add up to.
  • Above it, the tools treat the capacitors' mounting differently. Each capacitor reaches the planes through its pads, a fanout trace (mostly 0.25 mm, on B.Cu or F.Cu, 0.48 mm from the nearest plane) and a via. HyperLynx's capacitor models have INCLUDES_MOUNTING=1: the 0.40 and 0.58 nH are taken as the whole mounted inductance, and HyperLynx leaves that copper out of the loop. KiPI takes the ESL as the part's own and field-solves the mounting on top, so its first series resonance is lower (0.86 against 1.14 MHz) and it reads 0.63 against 0.31 nH above 100 MHz.
  • With the mounting left out of KiPI too, the inductance matches. Moving each capacitor's pads onto its nearest via of the same net, on every layer that via has (92 pads), attaches the parts at the planes. KiPI then reads 0.29 nH at 100–300 MHz against HyperLynx's 0.31 nH, its first resonance falls at 1.14 against 1.14 MHz, and the resistive floor at 300 MHz is 8.9 against 9.0 mΩ (the green curve).
  • What still differs: 2–10 MHz. Here the capacitor banks resonate against each other, and how high the anti-resonances rise depends on the series resistance around them. At 10 MHz HyperLynx reads 19 mΩ, KiPI 28 mΩ, and KiPI with the capacitors at their vias 112 mΩ. Attaching at the vias also removes the pads' and traces' resistance, which damps those peaks, and "nearest via" is only an approximation of each part's mounting. So this part of the curve isn't a like-for-like comparison yet.
  • One error in KiPI, fixed since the first comparison (#258). Copper narrower than about twice its height over its return had too much inductance: a 0.25 mm trace 0.48 mm over ground solved to 1.25 nH/mm, where a microstrip's is 0.55. Such traces now get the microstrip's inductance. At U30 this took +3V3 from 0.67 to 0.63 nH and +1V2 from 0.81 to 0.75 nH. The KiPI curves here include the fix.
  • Not the mesh. Refining to a 0.5 mm maximum edge and 0.015 mm minimum, everywhere on the board (858,238 triangles), changes KiPI's 0.63 nH by 2 %.
  • +1V2 needs a new HyperLynx run. HyperLynx's +1V2 result comes from the first .hyp export, which lacks U36's and U8's thermal vias. +1V2 is also mostly traces, so attaching its capacitors at their vias would short out rail copper as well as mounting. It isn't compared here.
Comparing the two tools. For a like-for-like run, either give KiPI each part's own ESL (without mounting), or set HyperLynx to compute the mounting from the geometry instead of INCLUDES_MOUNTING=1.
