The same glasgow.hyp board and the same BUD (6 VRMs, 8 series parts, 63 DC sink pins) solved in both tools. ΔV is KiPI minus HyperLynx at each pin HyperLynx reports.

What the comparison shows

±0.07 mVLargest difference on rails without series parts (+3V3, +1V2 core, VIO_AUX). HyperLynx reports to 0.1 mV.
2.65 mVLargest raw difference, at the +5V LDO inputs, downstream of FB1 and U15.
×1.0229HyperLynx's resistance for every series part relative to the BUD's RDC (spread ±0.0003).
±0.10 mVLargest difference over all 82 power pins once KiPI's link resistances are scaled by 1.023.
  • The copper solutions agree. Every remaining difference traces to the series parts, where HyperLynx's resistance is 2.3 % higher than the RDC the BUD gives. That is uniform across the board, as if every part were warmed by 5.8 °C at copper's TCR. KiPI uses RDC exactly.
  • The ground models differ. HyperLynx solves each VRM tree separately and returns LDO input current to J1. KiPI fixes every VRM ground at 0 V in one solve. Ground-pin voltages differ by at most 0.4 mV, but the return-current paths are very different.
  • Thermal results are on the KiPI side only so far: peak 31.7 °C at FB1 from 53.5 mW.

Key pins

Series parts

HyperLynx's resistance here is its reported drop divided by KiPI's current. The two tools' currents agree to HyperLynx's 4-digit resolution. The temperature is KiPI's mean over each part's pads.

The ratio is the same for parts at 25.6 °C and at 30.9 °C in KiPI's map, so HyperLynx is not scaling by local temperature. Two readings fit: it scales every RDC by copper TCR at one board-wide temperature (about 25.8 °C against a 20 °C reference), or it applies a fixed passive temperature. KiPI warms only SPICE-model links, so plain resistances stay at RDC. HyperLynx's passive temperature setting should settle which reading is right.

Ground and return current

HyperLynx sends the whole 0.340 A back to J1, and each LDO ground pin returns its input current as a sink. KiPI holds every VRM ground at 0 V in a single GND solve, so most of the return goes to the nearest LDO ground: 0.043 A reaches J1, U31 and U14 take about 0.17 A each, and J5.43 takes 34 mA for a 5 mA rail. Power-net voltages barely move, but ground current density and loss differ. This comes from modelling the LDOs as ideal sources with fixed grounds.

Via currents

HyperLynx reports each layer-to-layer segment; KiPI one current per via. The 0.1 A entries at J2 and J3 are the connector's plated pin holes, which KiPI treats as pads.

Thermal

Per-part temperatures come from a KiPI run of the same setup on the .kicad_pcb; it matches the .hyp run to 0.01 mV and 0.01 °C peak. HyperLynx temperature results have not been posted yet.
